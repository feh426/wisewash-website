The engines

Three engines. One verified file.

Each engine reads a site’s own point-of-sale, membership and billing data, and every figure it produces traces to an input, a benchmark with a cited source, or an override recorded in an audit register.

Engine 01

Membership Value Verifier

What the membership base is actually worth.

Membership is the largest single driver of an express wash’s value and the hardest to see from a report. The verifier reads the roster, the wash log, the billing history and the point-of-sale audit log, and tests every row.

Used by lenders before funding, buyers and note buyers, and owners preparing a sale or a refinance.

Verify a membership base

What it finds

  • Duplicate accounts and plates with no active vehicle
  • Non-transactional and dormant accounts still counted as members
  • Paused accounts and churn that has not yet surfaced
  • Promotional spikes and price step-up overhang
  • Conversion and back-office adjustments

What it produces

  • Verified member count and collectible recurring revenue
  • Lifetime value by plan and commitment length, using Kaplan-Meier survival curves
  • A value bridge from the reported base to the verified base
  • An Excel verification pack a lender or accountant can open and test
Engine 02

Operating Improvement Engine

Where the next dollar of earnings comes from.

The engine scores a site or a portfolio against published benchmarks, names each area of concern, and builds a strategic plan for every lever with the earnings it is worth, the ramp and the payback. Every input, benchmark and assumption can be overridden, and the change flows through the whole plan.

Used by owners and operators, platforms, buyers after closing, and lenders stabilizing an asset.

Score a site

The levers

  • Dunning, churn and membership conversion
  • Pricing, plan structure and retail ticket
  • Labor and each cost line
  • Water reclaim, capacity and throughput
  • Marketing

What it produces

  • A benchmark scorecard for the site or the portfolio
  • Areas of concern ranked by the earnings at stake
  • A sequenced plan with anticipated results, ramp and payback for each lever
Engine 03

Wash Deal Engine

Sixteen modules carry a site from raw point-of-sale exports to a supportable value, a financeable capital structure and a written record of every assumption behind them. It includes the membership verification and the operating plan, and adds the trade area, valuation, structure and risk.

Underwrite a site

What it covers

  • Membership verification at row level
  • Trade-area capture inside three and five miles, with the permit pipeline weighted
  • P&L normalization, with add-backs credited by category
  • Current and future value on sourced multiples
  • Capital structure and returns across the full term
  • Scenarios, Monte Carlo, a risk register and an override register

What it produces

  • An underwriting report and an Excel underwriting workbook
  • A supportable value and a walk-away number
  • A lender package in the order a credit committee reads
  • Side-by-side comparison and a portfolio roll-up across sites

Each engine reads the site’s own exports. We name the exact files to pull for DRB Patheon and SiteWatch, Sonny's, Washify, ICS, Micrologic, NXT and AMP, so nothing arrives as a summary. Same inputs, same result, on any machine, and your lender can rerun the file and reach our answer.

Reviewed line by line in a multi-pass audit. Nine material defects were found and corrected in that review, including one that would have overstated lender capacity by roughly three million dollars on a single site.

  • The collectible base differed from the headline countRow-level inspection found duplicate accounts, subscriptions with no vehicle attached, accounts outside the dormancy window and cards already failing. The collectible base came in 8.5% below the stated figure.
  • Churn needed a common basisReported churn had been netted against expected recovery. Measured on the same basis as the published industry blend, it ran above benchmark.
  • Add-backs were credited by categoryOwner compensation, one-time items and personal expenses each carry different evidence. Credited by category, they came to 28.8% of earnings.
  • The competitive pipeline was pricedA national brand under construction 1.1 miles out, weighted by how likely it is to open and phased over the years it opens.
  • Coverage was tested past year oneCoverage passed during the interest-only period and tightened once amortization began. A year-one test alone would have cleared a structure that does not hold.

For loan originators and the capital markets

Car wash collateral, verified before you fund and repositioned if it stops performing.

An appraisal values the real estate. In a car wash loan, much of the value also sits in the membership base and the staffed operation. We underwrite both before the note funds, and we protect both if the loan stops performing.

Before the loan closes

Pre-close verification

An independent test of the borrower's deal before you fund: the membership base at row level, normalized earnings, trade-area capture and the competitor pipeline, and debt service coverage across the full term rather than the interest-only year. Delivered in the order a credit committee reads, with a reliance letter to the lender.

A second read on a credit

A borrower projection or a workout plan that needs an operator’s eyes. We test the membership assumptions, the labor model and the trade area against what the site can produce, and we show where the plan holds and where it would need to change.

When a loan stops performing
01

Assess

A condition, value and going-concern report on a site you have taken back or are about to. We count and verify the membership base rather than assume it. Delivered in days.

02

Manage as-is

We keep the tunnel running and protect the members while you decide. Staff, vendors, chemical supply and merchant processing managed, with lender-grade monthly reporting. Under a private agreement, or alongside a court-appointed receiver.

03

Manage to improve

We correct the equipment faults, restart membership sales, fix pricing and plan structure, and take out labor waste, so the site returns to a number a buyer will underwrite.

04

Prepare for resale

The asset goes to market with an underwriting file built for the buyer pool, whether you sell the property or the note. Your broker runs the sale. Our fee is fixed and never a share of the price.

A membership base starts to cancel the week a tunnel goes dark. Most of the recovery on resale is decided in the months before the asset reaches market.

Across a book

Portfolio triage

Every car wash exposure in your book ranked by recoverable value and by how fast it is moving, with a recommended action for each site. You learn which files need attention this quarter and which do not.

Note and pool diligence

Real estate restructure and net lease

For owners, operators and buyers

Running your first wash, or buying your fifth.

Many first-time buyers come from outside the industry. The tunnel, the equipment and the lot can be inspected in an afternoon. The harder questions sit in the membership base, the add-backs and the trade area, and answering them takes someone who has run the business.

We work from the site’s point-of-sale, billing and roster exports, and we give you a supportable price, a walk-away number and a lender-ready file before you commit.

If you already own the site, the Operating Improvement Engine scores it against benchmark and prices every lever, so you know where the next dollar of earnings comes from and what it will take to hold it.

Five phases. Enter where your deal is.
01

Screen

Does this deal deserve diligence, and how far is the ask from a supportable price? A written go or no-go from the offering materials and whatever data has been released. One business day.

02

Verify

What is the asset worth on verified data, and where do you walk away? The full engine underwrite, with the report, the workbook and the price bridge. Two business days.

03

Structure

Which capital structure survives the full term? Debt sizing, coverage tested through amortization, SBA injection rules, and seller notes priced as the debt they are. One business day.

04

Operate

What will you do with it once you own it, and what is that plan worth? Each lever priced, sequenced and ramped, with its payback. Two business days.

05

Portfolio

Which sites, in what order, and what is the platform worth together? Side-by-side comparison and a roll-up priced at the tier the site count reaches. Two business days.

Each phase stands on its own. Phase 1 credits in full toward Phase 2 within 60 days, bundles reduce the total when phases are engaged together, and the clock starts when complete data arrives.

How an engagement runs

A defined protocol, not an opinion.

Every engagement runs the same eleven-phase accuracy protocol, with stop gates that halt the process when a figure cannot be tied out. From your side it runs in five steps.

01

Request

The exact reports to pull, named per point-of-sale system, so nothing arrives as a summary.

02

Reconcile

Three-way tie-out between the P&L, the point-of-sale export and the member roster before anything is valued.

03

Verify

Roster forensics, trade-area work on the ground, and competitor counts confirmed against permit records.

04

Underwrite

Normalized earnings, valuation, structure and stress testing, ending in a supportable price and a walk-away.

05

Act

The findings become the lender package, the capital structure, the operating plan or the negotiating position.

If a number cannot be tied to source data, it does not enter the model. It becomes a question we resolve with whoever produced it.
